象棋 一手

Learn Chinese chess in five minutes.

xiàng · qí · the elephant game
I. 棋盤
棋盤 The Board

Two armies face each other across a river. Pieces sit on the intersections, not inside the squares — nine files wide, ten ranks deep, split into a red half and a black half.

FilesNine vertical lines. Pieces move along them as on a roadway.
RanksTen horizontal lines, split in the middle by the river.
RiverA wide empty band — 楚河漢界. Some pieces may not cross; others change when they do.
PalaceThe 3×3 fortress at each end, marked with an X. Two pieces stay inside it all game.
PointsEach side has 16 pieces — 32 in total — placed on intersections at the start.
II. 棋子
棋子 The Seven Pieces

Seven kinds of piece per side. Black uses one set of characters; Red uses a mirrored set with the same meanings. Learning the moves is learning the game.

將 ／ 帥

General 將 · 帥

jiàng / shuài — the king

Your general is the piece you must keep alive. If it falls, the game is over. In exchange for that importance, it barely moves.

  • MoveOne step along a line — up, down, left, or right. Never diagonally.
  • ConfinedStays inside its own palace for the entire game.
  • Strange ruleThe two generals may never face each other down an open file with nothing between them.
士 ／ 仕

Advisor 士 · 仕

shì / shì — the bodyguard

A quiet piece whose only job is to stand beside the general and catch checks. Bodyguards in stone shoes.

  • MoveOne step diagonally — nothing else.
  • ConfinedNever leaves the palace. It only ever visits five points.
象 ／ 相

Elephant 象 · 相

xiàng / xiāng — the minister

The elephant moves in a graceful diagonal leap of exactly two points. It has two weaknesses: it cannot cross the river, and it can be blocked.

  • MoveTwo points diagonally — a perfect square's jump, any direction.
  • Blocked eyeIf the midway intersection is occupied, the elephant may not jump that way.
  • River-boundAn elephant stays on its own side forever. It defends; it never invades.
馬 ／ 傌

Horse 馬 · 傌

mǎ / mǎ — the knight

The horse makes the familiar L–shape — but unlike Western chess, it does not jump. One step straight, then one step diagonally outward. If the straight step is blocked, the horse is hobbled in that direction.

  • MoveOne step orthogonal, then one step diagonal outward — eight possible squares.
  • HobblingA piece on the adjacent orthogonal square removes both L’s on that side.
車 ／ 俥

Chariot 車 · 俥

jū / jū — the rook

The strongest piece on the board. The chariot rolls any distance along its file or rank, so long as its path is clear. Same as the Western rook.

  • MoveAny number of empty points in a straight line — never diagonally.
  • CaptureThe first enemy piece in its path; it cannot leap.
砲 ／ 炮

Cannon 砲 · 炮

pào / pào — the gunpowder piece

The cannon moves exactly like a chariot — until it captures. To take an enemy piece, it must leap over exactly one piece in between. That piece is called the screen.

  • Quiet moveAny distance along a line, path clear — just like a chariot.
  • CaptureMust jump over exactly one piece (friend or foe) and land on an enemy.
  • No screen, no captureTwo pieces in between, or none — no capture is possible.
卒 ／ 兵

Soldier 卒 · 兵

zú / bīng — the pawn

Five soldiers march forward at the start — and they can never retreat. They grow up when they cross the river: sideways movement unlocks. There is no promotion; an old soldier in the ninth rank still moves one step at a time.

  • Before riverOne step forward — only forward.
  • After riverOne step forward or one step sideways.
  • Never backA soldier does not retreat, ever.
III. 走棋
走棋 A Turn

Red moves first. You must move — there is no passing. Each turn is a single piece to a single destination.

吃子

Capture

Landing on an enemy piece captures it and removes it from the board. You can never land on your own piece.

將軍

Check

If your next move threatens to capture the enemy general, you are giving check. Announcing it aloud is a courtesy, not a rule.

應將

Getting out

On your next turn you must escape the check — by moving the general, blocking the line, or capturing the attacker.

無路

No legal move

Every move must leave your general safe. A move that exposes your own general to capture is illegal.

IV. 特例
特例 Three Odd Rules

Xiangqi carries three rules that surprise beginners. Each is structural — not a footnote, but a basic shape of the game.

將帥對面

Flying general

The two generals may never see each other down an empty file. If nothing blocks the line between them, the position is illegal — and in theory, one general could fly to capture the other.

不變作負

No perpetual check

You may not repeatedly give check with the same attacker in the same way. Forcing a draw by endless check means you lose.

長捉不變

No perpetual chase

Repeatedly threatening to capture the same piece with the same attacker is also forbidden. The chaser must vary the attack or give up the chase.

V. 勝負
勝負 To Win

There are two ways to win and one way to draw. Unlike Western chess, a player with no legal move does not draw — they lose.

Win · I

將死 checkmate

The enemy general is in check and cannot escape — can’t move, can’t block, can’t capture the attacker. The game ends at once.

Win · II

困斃 stalemate

Your opponent has no legal move at all, even though they are not in check. In xiangqi, that is not a draw — they have lost.

A game can also end by resignation, by agreement on a drawn position, or by the arbiter’s move-count rules in tournament play. Perpetual check and perpetual chase, as above, cost the offender the game.

That’s the whole game.

Red opens. Seven pieces, two palaces, one river between them. The rest is a thousand years of study — and your first hundred moves.
